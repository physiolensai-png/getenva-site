Where the data comes from

Everything feeds the same four scores.

Apple Watch, workouts, labs, supplements, nutrition, sleep — none of these live in separate apps with separate conclusions. In Enva they are all inputs to a single model. The scores don't compete with each other. They are downstream of the same data, asking different questions from it.

Health Score

The number you check first. Every morning.

Health Score is the front door. It answers the question no single metric can: how well are you actually doing across all dimensions this week? Not just recovered, not just active — trained well, slept well, trending in the right direction.

A detraining week shows a low Health Score even when Recovery is high. Hard training with poor sleep shows a low score even when you feel like you pushed. That is the point — it catches the things your intuition flatters.

Recovery

Calibrates your effort for the day.

Recovery is not about rest. It is about whether your body is in a position to absorb today's work. High Recovery on a rest day is exactly right. Low Recovery after a hard block does not mean something is wrong — it means the training is doing its job, and you should match today's effort to what the body can currently handle.

HRV

HRV at wake

The strongest single predictor of autonomic recovery. Enva uses your full sleep-period average rather than a pre-wake spike, which removes the artificial inflation that makes other apps report 73% when you feel like 40%.

Sleep

Last night's quality

Recovery starts with Sleep Score. Deep sleep is growth hormone and tissue repair. REM is cognitive consolidation. Duration matters too — stage proportions that look healthy on a six-hour night do not score the same as on eight.

Load

Yesterday's training

Effort above your 30-day average reduces Recovery. Effort below it adds a rest-day boost. The adjustment is gated: if wrist temp signals physiological stress, the rest boost is suppressed — rest days look different from sick days.

Sleep Score

One of the most important inputs in the system.

Sleep Score feeds both Recovery and Health Score directly. A bad week of sleep depresses all three. The score is built from what the night actually produced — not just whether you logged eight hours — because duration without the right stage distribution is not the same thing as good sleep.

What the score is measuring

Deep sleep and REM are scored relative to your own 30-day baseline, not a population chart. A user whose deep sleep average is 45 minutes reaches 100 at 45 minutes. Absolute duration is also weighted — stage proportions alone cannot produce a perfect score on a short night.

HRV, resting heart rate, and wrist temperature round out the picture. Wrist temp is confirmatory: a deviation above 0.5 degrees signals physiological stress and suppresses Recovery's rest-day boost, regardless of what the sleep stages show.

Biological Age

This is where the work shows up.

The first three scores tell you how you are doing now. Biological Age tells you whether those efforts are producing actual physiological adaptation. Zone 2 work, consistent sleep, sufficient protein — it takes months for these to register in VO2 Max, body composition, and recovery markers. Biological Age is the score that captures them.

It is not a quarterly check-in. It moves continuously as your data accumulates. A four-week training block, a period of poor sleep, a nutrition shift — all of it lands in Biological Age eventually.

How Biological Age is calculated

Five weighted domains, your own data, and a confidence range instead of a false-precision number. Full methodology on the Biological Age page.

Reading the System

When the scores disagree, that is the signal.

Four scores reading in the same direction is confirmation. Four scores pointing different ways is information — it tells you which part of the system to look at. Disagreement is not a bug. It is the most useful thing the system can show you.

Recovery Health Score What it means
High Low Well-rested but the week's training was not productive. Check zone balance and training consistency in the Load Quality pillar.
Low High Hard block accumulating. Normal and expected during productive overreach. Match today's effort to Recovery, not to how the week is trending.
High Sleep Low Recovery The sleep stages look good but HRV is below baseline. A subclinical stressor — travel, illness onset, accumulated stress — is showing up in the autonomic signal before anywhere else.
High High Both scores aligned. Your readiness and your weekly training quality are in the same place. Good week to push if Biological Age is trending the same direction.
In Practice

Reading a real day with all four scores.

Tuesday morning. Sleep Score 71 — shorter night, deep sleep at 85% of baseline. Recovery 63 — usable, not optimal. Health Score 78 — solid week of training even with the poor night. Biological Age holding at 34.2.

Step 01

Check Recovery first

63 means you are ready to train but not to push a new threshold. A moderate session — zone 2, technique work, or lighter strength — fits the day. A PR attempt does not.

Step 02

Health Score gives context

78 tells you the week has been productive despite last night. One bad night is not a bad week. You do not need to compensate for it — you need to not let it become a pattern.

Step 03

Biological Age confirms direction

Holding steady at 34.2 with a chronological age of 39. The adaptation is real. The goal now is to keep the inputs that produced it consistent — not to optimize today's individual session.

Goals

A declared goal changes what the scores are telling you.

All four scores acquire context when you have a target. A Hyrox finish time, a Dri-Tri, a body composition goal — setting it tells Enva what the scores are optimizing for. Recovery becomes readiness for that event. Health Score becomes a measure of whether this week's training is building toward it. Biological Age becomes the long-term confirmation that the approach is working.

Without a goal the scores describe your health. With one, they tell you whether you're on track.
