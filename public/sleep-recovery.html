What goes in

Measured against your baseline, not a population chart.

A person whose deep sleep averages 45 minutes should not be scored against someone who averages 80. Enva learns your 30 to 60-day baseline for each signal and scores the night relative to it, with absolute duration still weighted so stage proportions alone cannot carry a short night.

Deep sleep

Growth hormone and tissue repair. Scored against your own baseline, with a floor on absolute minutes.

REM sleep

Cognitive consolidation. Weighted alongside deep sleep rather than lumped into a single "quality" figure.

Duration & consistency

Total time asleep and how steady your schedule is. Irregularity carries its own penalty.

HRV at wake

The strongest single predictor of autonomic recovery. Enva uses the full sleep-period average, not a pre-wake spike, which is why the number matches how you feel instead of flattering it.

Resting heart rate

Overnight low against your baseline. A sustained rise is one of the earliest signs of accumulated stress or illness.

Wrist temperature

Confirmatory. A deviation above 0.5 ° signals physiological stress and suppresses the rest-day boost, regardless of what the sleep stages show.

How they relate

Sleep feeds Recovery. Recovery calibrates the day.

Low Recovery is not a warning

Recovery is not about rest. It is about whether your body is in a position to absorb today's work. High Recovery on a rest day is exactly right. Low Recovery after a hard block means the training is doing its job. Today's effort should match what the body can currently handle.

Yesterday's load above your 30-day average lowers the score. A genuine rest day adds a boost. If wrist temperature signals stress, that boost is suppressed. Rest days and sick days should not look the same.
