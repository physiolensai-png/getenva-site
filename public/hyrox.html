The Problem

Nothing you use measures compromised running.

Your running watch sees the runs. Your gym app sees the strength work. Neither sees the thing that decides the race — what your legs do on run six, after the sled and the burpee broad jumps. The number that matters is not your 1k time. It is your 1k time when you are already wrecked.

Split training, split data

Runs in one app, stations in another, and no single view that knows how one is eating into the other.

Volume without direction

More sessions is not a plan. Without a target time nothing tells you whether this week moved you closer or further away.

Recovery treated as separate

Sleep and fuel are not a wellness sidebar. They are inputs to the finish time, and they belong in the same model as the training.

How It Works

One target. Every input that feeds it.

You set the time you want and the date you want it. From there Enva stops reporting numbers at you and starts answering one question instead: what is holding this back right now.

Zone distribution
Whether your easy work is genuinely easy. Hyrox punishes an aerobic base built entirely at threshold, and most people training for it are living in the wrong zone.
Recovery
HRV, resting heart rate and how fast your heart drops after effort — read against your own baseline, not a population chart.
Sleep
Duration, consistency, and the deep and REM split. The part that determines whether last week's training turned into fitness or just fatigue.
Nutrition
Calories against your real expenditure, and whether protein is actually supporting the strength side while you hold a deficit.
Load balance
Running against stations against everything else. Progressive overload only works when the progression is visible.
Labs
Ferritin, vitamin D, thyroid, inflammatory markers. When the training is right and the times still are not moving, this is usually where the answer is.
