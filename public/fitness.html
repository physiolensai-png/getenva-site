What Enva keeps

The shape of your training, not just the total.

Volume alone does not tell you much. An athlete who only ever goes hard and one who balances intensity can log the same hours and adapt completely differently. Enva scores the distribution.

Zone distribution

Minutes in each heart-rate zone per week, so you can see whether your easy days are actually easy and your hard days are hard enough.

Training load

TRIMP across your sessions, tracked against a rolling baseline. This is the number that drives the Health Score's Training Load pillar and Recovery's day-after adjustment.

Strength frequency & volume

Sessions per week, sets and load per movement, with left and right sides tracked separately where it matters.

VO₂ Max trend

Corrected per activity type: outdoor runs use the watch's native estimate, intervals use per-session heart rate. Then trended over six weeks rather than reported as a single noisy number.

Training variety

How many distinct modalities you touch. Monotony is a risk factor the Activity Profile domain scores directly.

Platform detail

Peloton output, cadence, resistance, and leaderboard rank. Orangetheory splat points, rower 500m splits, and tread pace. Kept in full, not reduced to a calorie estimate.

Where it connects

The input every output reacts to.

Health Score

Training Load is one of the three pillars. Zone balance and strength frequency determine whether a big week reads as productive or as overreaching.

Sleep & Recovery

Yesterday's load above your 30-day average lowers today's Recovery; a genuine rest day adds a boost, gated by wrist temperature.

Biological Age

Cardiovascular Fitness and Activity Profile together carry half the model. This is the input with the most leverage on the long-term number.
