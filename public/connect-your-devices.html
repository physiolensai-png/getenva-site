Setup guide

Connect your devices the right way.

When two devices record the same class, apps count it twice or lose its heart rate. A few minutes of setup fixes that. Pick the one device that records each workout, then let Enva add the rest.

The one rule

One device records. Everything else adds.

Apple Health is where the workout and its heart rate live. Enva reads them from there. Your Orangetheory and Peloton connections add what Apple Health has no place for, and never replace what your watch or strap recorded.

  • Recorded by one deviceHeart rate, calories and duration come from the Apple Health workout.
  • Added by OrangetheorySplat points, heart rate zones, studio, coach, and treadmill and rower detail.
  • Added by PelotonOutput, cadence, resistance, speed, distance and leaderboard rank.
  • Matched by time, not by typeA Tread 50 logged as a run, a Strength 50 logged as strength and a class logged as Other all match, because the clock times overlap.
Pick your setup

Three ways that work.

Setup 1

Apple Watch for everything

Start the workout on your watch. Connect your Orangetheory and Peloton accounts in Enva and the class details attach to the watch workout on their own.

Best forAnyone who wears a watch in class and wants the least to manage.
Setup 2

Orangetheory with an OTbeat Link

The OTbeat Link clips to your Apple Watch band and pairs only to the watch. Allow the Orangetheory app to read Apple Health, and start an Orangetheory workout on the watch. The watch is still the recorder. Enva adds splats and zones from your account.

Setup 3

Peloton with a watch or strap

The Peloton app can save workouts and heart rate to Apple Health, and can use an Apple Watch for heart rate. Choose one: the watch records the workout, or Peloton writes it. Not both.

CheckIn Health, only one app can write Workouts for the same ride.
Heart rate takes a detour

When a monitor feeds another app first.

Some setups send heart rate through a second app before it reaches Apple Health, for example a strap that feeds Peloton, which then saves to Health. That can work well. It goes wrong only when your watch records the same session as well. Five steps settle it.

  1. Choose the recorder for this workout. One device or one app. If your watch is on your wrist, it is the easiest choice.
  2. Check who can write to Health. In Apple Health, tap your profile picture, then Apps and Services. Open each fitness app and leave Workouts and Heart Rate on only for the recorder.
  3. Connect Apple Health in Enva. Open Settings, then Devices and Data, then Connections, and connect Apple Health.
  4. Add Orangetheory, Peloton or Oura. Connect each one on the same Connections screen. Orangetheory and Peloton add class detail. Oura adds sleep and daily metrics.
  5. Set your device preferences. Once a ring and a watch are both connected, Device Preferences appears under Devices and Data. Choose which one leads for sleep, daily metrics and workouts.
Common questions

Setup, answered.

Why does the same class show up twice in Apple Health?
Two apps saved it. If your Apple Watch records the class and the Peloton or Orangetheory app also writes a workout to Apple Health, Health keeps both. Open Health, tap your profile picture, choose Apps and Services, open the app you do not want recording, and turn off Workouts and Heart Rate under its write permissions. Enva keeps one record when two workouts start close together and run about the same length, but turning off the extra writer is what gives you one clean heart rate line.
My Tread 50 or Strength 50 is not a HIIT workout in Apple Health. Will it still match?
Yes. Enva matches Orangetheory and Peloton classes to an Apple Health workout by clock time, not by workout type. A Tread 50 your watch logged as a run, a Strength 50 logged as strength training and a class logged as Other all match the same way, as long as the times overlap.
Which heart rate does Enva use when two devices disagree?
Apple Health's. Enva reads heart rate and calories from the Health workout first. Orangetheory and Peloton only fill in a number when Health has none, so your connection never overwrites what your watch or strap recorded.
Do I have to connect my Orangetheory or Peloton account?
No. Apple Health alone gives you the workout, the heart rate and the calories. Connecting an account adds what Health cannot hold: splat points, heart rate zones, studio and coach for Orangetheory, and output, cadence, resistance and leaderboard rank for Peloton.
My heart rate comes from a monitor that feeds another app before it reaches Apple Health. Does that work?
It can, as long as one app is the recorder. Whatever the chain, the workout reaches Enva as an Apple Health workout, so check which apps are allowed to write Workouts and Heart Rate in Health and leave on only the one you want recording that session. If two apps write the same session, you will see two heart rate sources for one class.
Can I use an Oura Ring and an Apple Watch together?
Yes. When both are connected, Enva sets sleep and daily metrics to your ring and workouts to your watch by default. You can change each one under Settings, then Devices and Data, then Device Preferences, which appears once both are connected. Enva never blends two devices within the same night or day.
Do I need an Apple Watch?
No. Enva works with an Apple Watch, an Oura Ring or neither. For workout heart rate you need something that writes heart rate to Apple Health, such as a watch or a Bluetooth strap paired to an app that saves to Health. You can also log workouts by hand.
