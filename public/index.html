Reading the system

When the outputs disagree, that is the signal.

Every readout pointing the same way is confirmation. Readouts pointing different ways is information, and it tells you which input to look at. That is only possible when they all sit in one system.

Recovery Health Score What it means
High Low Well-rested, but the week's training was not productive. Look at zone balance and training consistency.
Low High A hard block is accumulating. Normal during productive overreach. Match today's effort to Recovery, not to how the week is trending.
Sleep fine Recovery low Stages look good but HRV is below baseline. A stressor, such as travel, illness onset, or accumulated load, is showing up in the autonomic signal first.
Why this is hard today

Today, your data lives in many apps.

A sleep app knows your sleep. A training app knows your workouts. A nutrition app knows your meals. Your lab portal knows your blood work. None of them talk to each other. Enva brings them together in one place, so your inputs can actually produce outputs, instead of you screenshotting everything into a chatbot to get one coherent read.

Each app sees one slice

Every platform reads its own data and stops there. None can tell you how your training is affecting your sleep, or how your nutrition is affecting your recovery.

Labs sit in a silo

Blood work arrives as a PDF nobody explains. It never connects to your sleep, training load, or recovery patterns, where it would actually mean something.

You do the integration by hand

Copy-pasting numbers between apps to assemble a picture is work the software should be doing. Enva does it, and keeps doing it every day.

What makes it work

Apple Health flattens your data to a generic "workout." Enva connects directly to each ecosystem and keeps what they actually capture: Peloton output and leaderboard rank, Orangetheory splat points, rower splits and tread pace, not just a label and a duration.
